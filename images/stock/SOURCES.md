# Stock Photo Sources — Al-Hamza Caterers Website

All photographs in `images/stock/` are **illustrative catering visuals** used for
website banners, service cards and occasion cards. They are **not** photographs of
actual Al-Hamza Caterers events. Real Al-Hamza food, buffet and event photographs
remain in `images/` (original business photos) and the 47 event films in `videos/`.

Every photo below is sourced from **Pexels** (https://www.pexels.com/license/ —
free to use, no attribution required; credit given here voluntarily). Photos were
downloaded locally and converted to WebP. No remote hotlinking is used anywhere.

## October 2026 refresh — Indian-catering visual correction

The 12 `-indian.webp` files below replaced the previous generic/foreign-looking
banners and service-card photos. Each replacement was **visually inspected**
(contact sheet verified) for Indian-catering relevance: no foreign wedding
couples, no Western banquet halls, no non-Indian dishes, no watermarks, no
competitor branding. Rejected candidates during research (branded menu card,
Southeast-Asian stir-fry, Baltic forest wedding, half-empty canteen trays,
momos platter, oddly-styled chelo plate) were **not** used.

The homepage hero (`hero-home.webp`, Zak Chapman) was left **unchanged**.
The founder photo (`images/founder.jpeg`), logo and all real gallery photos
and 47 videos were left untouched.

## October 2026 refresh (part 2) — occasion-card visual correction

The six foreign-looking occasion-card photos below were replaced with
**visually inspected** North Indian / Mughlai catering photographs. Each
replacement file was opened and checked at full size: unambiguously Indian
dishes (biryani, handi curries, naan, tikka, tandoori, dal makhani), no
foreign wedding couples, no Western banquet halls, no Western cuisine, no
watermarks, no competitor branding, sharp premium quality. One candidate
(paneer dish with a visible "biryani tree" restaurant card, Pexels 9609838)
was deliberately **rejected and not used**. `occasion-family.webp` (dates,
gulab jamun, sharbat — appropriate for Indian Muslim family daawats) and
`occasion-festive.webp` (chicken curry with rice — Indian festive feast)
were visually re-checked and **retained**.
